The page

  • One line per category: its name, last month's actual spending printed in mono (D47), and a box to write this month's limit in. A box left empty keeps last month's figure, so a quiet month costs no writing.
  • The boxes are the only places the reader looks. Their positions are fixed in one place in code and shared by the page that draws them and the prompt that reads them, the way Foreword's journal page keeps its two writing regions (JournalRegions). A figure written outside a box is ignored.
  • A box the reader can't make out is sent back on next month's page for checking, never guessed (D49, mockup 8).
  • New categories can be written in (D151): three blank lines at the foot, each a name box and a limit box. A name the reader matches to an existing category is treated as that line; a new name becomes a category in Index (and in Settings) with the limit written beside it. A name with no limit, or one it can't read, is sent back to check (D49). Three lines is a guess at enough; the page stays one sheet.
  • The total at the top is the month's expected income (D155), not last month's spending added up: it's the number the limits are written against. It's projected from past paychecks, not from what happens to land in the month (D102), and scoped to the accounts this install sees (D104). A box beside it takes a correction; a figure written there wins for the month.
  • Left to assign is a target, not a verdict: income, less the month's Snowball payments (the sheet's weekly total times the Fridays that fall in the month, D134), less last month's spending. It tells you roughly how much room there is while you write. Index works out the real remainder from what's written, including a corrected income, so the printed figure going stale is harmless.
  • Payments count Fridays; income is averaged (D102). A five-Friday month like October reads tighter than a four-Friday one because the payments really do come out five times, while income is smoothed across the year. That's accurate, but it's worth knowing when the target looks small.
  • When Index can't tell (irregular deposits, D103), the figure is left off and the line reads “Not sure — write it in”, so the box is the only source. Nothing is guessed onto the page.
  • Every figure is whole dollars, rounded down (D154). The limits' total is worked out in Index from what's written, so a slip in arithmetic on paper can't disagree with the app.
  • This month's page replaces last month's on the reMarkable (D60), and its arrival is silent (D89).
  • Printed in the muted document palette: ink, hairlines, the rule in the muted green. No texture behind the writing boxes, so e-ink and handwriting both stay clean (§11).

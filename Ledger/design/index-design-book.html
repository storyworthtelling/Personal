The cloth and the identity are one hue, which is Post's construction (identity read from the binding) rather than Folio's (a red cloth unrelated to an olive identity). One green across the book, the tab and the cover, per D106.

This is a departure from §3, and it is the only green there is

§3 binds every identity to ≥ 20° from every chromatic role. The painting's green sits 0° from Positive (129°). There is no green that passes: Positive (129°) and Action (152°) are 23° apart, so nothing between them clears both; below them is Folio's olive (107°); above them is Score's teal (178°). The whole wheel has three windows left — 227–233°, 305–306°, 346–357° — a blue and two pinks. The amber I proposed in the first draft of this screen was also wrong: it sat 15° from Foreword's 47°, which I had left out of the check.

What the departure costs, measured: Index's tiers are 2.6–4.6 ΔE (OKLab ×100) from Positive's — visibly grayer side by side, the same green apart. So Index's pages draw no Positive role: a paid-off loan is settled, which is the Resolved role's meaning and D12's crossed-out treatment anyway; an under-limit budget line needs no colour at all (D48 only colours overspend). The primary button stays Action pine at 152°, 23° clear.

Guards, written first: the suite's hue-separation guard gets a named exception for Index against Positive only (it still fails on any other role or any sibling), and a new Index guard fails if any Index page resolves a Positive token.

What's on the page

  • Every account is an index card lying on the graph paper: note-card stock, the account's name above a red head rule, pale blue rules below. The due line and the balance are written on the rules, never across one; measured, a blue rule drops soft ink to 4.34:1 and muted ink to 2.82:1 where it crosses a glyph. No shadow (§5), only a hairline edge. The bank's name sits on the graph paper above its cards.
  • When is it due? Every card and loan row puts its next due date and payment under its name, in soft ink where the account type used to be. The next payment comes out from behind the tap (this reverses part of D19). APR and the transactions stay behind the chevron, which is mockup 3.
  • Can I afford it? No computed number. A bank account shows its balance; a card shows its balance with its limit under it (“of $23,000”), both in mono, right-aligned.
  • Banks in the owner's order. Tap a bank's heading (frame 2) for a centred action card: move up, move down, move to the bottom, disconnect. There's no drag, the same as the Snowball stepper (D43). A new bank joins at the bottom, and the default order is alphabetical.
  • Paid-off accounts disappear from this page. Cedar Hollow Bank and Northfield Student Loans are gone, and a bank with nothing left open goes with them. The crossed-out settled treatment (D12) now belongs to Snowball only.
  • Tabs, one colour each, as Score and Post ship them: Accounts takes the cover green, Snowball the in-progress teal, Budget the caution ochre, Savings the reference slate-blue, and Settings the neutral gray. The gutter is filled with the active tab's cloth, which is BinderTabs.UpdateGutterColor's behaviour. No page wears its own tab's role colour; in-page accents stay green (AreaHueTests).
  • Graph paper. Green lines in the identity ground colour, a 16dp square with a heavier line every 64dp. The heavier line is 14% strong, the measured ceiling: muted ink holds 3.03:1 and soft ink 4.67:1 where a line crosses them. Any darker and both fall below their floors.
  • A broken connection keeps its figures and says “As of Sep 22” (D32), and the fix waits behind the dog-ear (§13). A hand-entered account says so on its heading (D08).

The chooser

  • A centred action card over the haze, the same component as the reorder card (§7). It isn't a dialog, and tapping outside it dismisses it.
  • Each choice says what it costs. Connecting needs Admin mode on this phone (D21), so the card says so before the owner taps, not after.

The form

  • The index card is the ground and the layout, nothing more. The fields and choosers are the design system's own (§7): boxed text fields on PaperBright, radius 12, and segmented controls for Kind and Every. The card gives them its red head rule and blue rules, and lines them up on those rules: each label sits on one rule, and each field or control is centred in the three rules below it, so a 44dp box keeps 11dp clear of the rule above and the rule below and never touches the lines. APR and Payment share their rules. The card is as large as the form needs and uses the full width of the page.
  • Only name and balance are required (D108). Their lines carry the faint identity tint §7 gives a required field. Everything else can be added later by tapping the card (D111), and an account without an APR and a payment stays out of the Snowball maths until it has both.
  • Bank or lender decides which heading the card sits under on Accounts (D118). Left blank, the account is its own heading.
  • Kind decides the card's second line: a card asks for its limit, a bank account needs no payment, and a loan (as here) needs neither a limit nor a mask.
  • New with this mockup: payment frequency and next due. D14 named a minimum or weekly payment; D125 now puts a due date on every loan's card, so a hand-entered loan needs one too. Every is week or month (the sheet pays weekly), and Next due opens §7's flip picker in place.
  • Figures in mono, in the mono variant of the field (§7). Validation happens on blur, then live; a bad figure turns the field's border rose with a line saying how to fix it.
  • Pinned bar: a quiet Cancel on the left, one primary Add account. Success is the card appearing on Accounts under Evergreen Home Lending, with no toast (§7). After that its balance can also come from Post (D15).

The states

  • Written on the page. A Serif heading and plain paragraphs on the graph paper; the index card is kept for the thing Index is about, an account.
  • Admin mode needed (frame 1). The page says why, where Admin mode is, and what doesn't need it. There's no button, because Index can't open Flyleaf's settings and Flyleaf isn't changing in this phase (D132). With no primary action there's no pinned bar (§7). The old instruction survives as a quiet fallback: connect on another phone, and the vault brings the bank here.
  • No timings in the copy (D133). How long Admin mode takes to open and how long it stays open are Flyleaf's to change, so no Index screen repeats them.
  • Admin mode open (frame 2). Three steps and one action. There's no Done button: coming back is enough, because Ledger already checks for a finished session when the page reappears.
  • Back from Chrome (frame 3). One teal status sentence while Plaid finishes (§6), then the new bank's cards appear on Accounts, with no toast. Check again is today's manual retry, now secondary.

Not verified

  • How Index knows Admin mode is open. Today's check asks which app is the default browser, and that stays the restricted viewer even in Admin mode, so it can't tell the two states apart. The new check would ask whether Chrome is suspended. That's readable for another package in principle, but unverified.
  • The bank sign-in hosts (D79) join Chrome's allowlist in the launcher's policy. A missing one shows Chrome's “blocked by your administrator” page, which Index never sees. This is the top on-device risk (D93).

The month

  • A budget line is an index card, the same card as an account (D141). Above the red rule, the category's name. On the blue rules, what's left and what's been spent, then a meter against the agreed limit.

The page

  • One line per category: its name, last month's actual spending printed in mono (D47), and a box to write this month's limit in. A box left empty keeps last month's figure, so a quiet month costs no writing.
  • The boxes are the only places the reader looks. Their positions are fixed in one place in code and shared by the page that draws them and the prompt that reads them, the way Foreword's journal page keeps its two writing regions (JournalRegions). A figure written outside a box is ignored.
  • A box the reader can't make out is sent back on next month's page for checking, never guessed (D49, mockup 8).
  • New categories can be written in (D151): three blank lines at the foot, each a name box and a limit box.   • Left to assign is a target, not a verdict: income, less the month's Snowball payments (the sheet's weekly total times the Fridays that fall in the month, D134), less last month's spending.

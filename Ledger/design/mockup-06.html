Two views, one switch

  • Loans | History, §7's segmented control, pinned under the header like the rest of the frame. The chosen side takes the highlighter wash in the identity green, not the tab's teal (§9). Snowball opens on Loans; the switch remembers the last side you chose.

The dates are the sheet's formulas

  • Reconstructed from the workbook and matched to all ten loans (D134), then written down in full in the Gate 2 finding, Appendix A, with the 19 Mar 2027 inputs and every expected output for the guard test.
  • The extra payment (D05, D73) is added to the focus loan's weekly payment and every date recalculates as you type. The sheet has none, so it starts at $0.00. It's also a line on the budget worksheet (D98).

The charts are the history

  • The sheet's two graphs, redrawn (D136) from the 70 recorded weeks, 2 Jun 2023 to 27 Sep 2024, imported once from Loans Over Time (the one interpolated week, 19 Jan 2024, kept as the sheet has it). From then on, Index adds one week at a time from live balances (D78).
  • Every loan, week by week: the sheet's stacked bars, one bar a week, one segment a loan. The shades run darkest to lightest in payoff order, so the segment disappearing next sits on top. It shows the real story: the Harbor auto loan arriving in December 2023, and the pile shrinking since.
  • Each loan: the sheet's per-loan lines, split into one small panel per loan, each on its own scale from $0 to that loan’s peak, so a loan’s progress is visible whatever its size. Ten lines in one 300dp chart would need ten colours, and the design system has no palette for that. Paid-off loans stay, ending at zero.
  • The mortgage is in both (D138): at the foot of the stacked bars, as on the sheet's live bar chart, and in its own panel. Its slow line is part of the story, not a scale problem, now that every loan has its own panel.
  • Neither chart is a role colour: the green ramp and ink-blue (D113). Touching a bar or a panel shows that week's date and balance.

The loans

  • Each card leads with now: payoff date and balance, then what the loan is paid each week today. The focus loan's figure already includes everything rolled down to it, $465.
  • The post-it holds what happens once it's the focus: the payment then, when it starts, what's left when it starts, and the sheet's weeks to payoff from there (D75).
  • Reorder by long-pressing a card (D43, D123); paid-off loans sit at the end, crossed out, gray (D12). No pinned bar: the extra payment is a field, not an action.
